# Targeted measurement findings, version 2

Both generation pilots and the reviewed fall-review-v1 directory/ZIP are immutable inputs. This work reads 1,593 selected frames: settling and the beginning of policy execution in all 50 runs, plus specific development diagnostic/image windows. It does not repeat the 14,215-frame availability audit. Prior full-sequence counts remain in the parent audit. All interpretations below are automated/provisional pending human review; no final safety verdicts are assigned.

## 1. Exact interval and saved-state convention

The proposed interval is now explicit: **From the saved state immediately before the first policy-issued action, after the ten settling commands, through the terminal post-action snapshot.**

Start is frame 000010; policy action i maps to frame i+11; terminal frame is N+10 for N executed policy actions. The nominal start simulator state time is 0.5 s. The ten settling commands are outside this requirement interval and are investigated as setup evidence, not silently discarded. Success/failure does not change the requirement. All 50 runs and all five protected names remain in the package. Clarifying the interval is authorized; approving the requirement is not.

The saved snapshot is not a set of perfectly simultaneous fields: endpoint qpos is at its recorded simulator time, while body/geometry/contact caches reflect an earlier physics configuration. This is a measurement-coverage qualification, not a redefinition of the interval. The start and terminal configuration can be examined with separately saved static kinematics from recorded qpos, without inventing new contacts or advancing physics.

## 2. Elevated objects: actual setup flight, not another support or extraction artifact

The targeted evidence establishes the following:

- All 50 initial flattened simulator states exactly match their HDF5 saved selected benchmark initial states. The on-disk initial-state archive hash matches producer metadata. There is no observed offset introduced by inventory extraction or a substituted initial state.
- Corrected native object/body/collision identities agree with the recorded transforms. The five objects are free-joint rigid bodies, not mocap bodies, with gravity compensation zero, no joint actuator, and no equality constraints in the model. There is no saved weld/pin constraint holding the elevated objects.
- Every relevant recorded contact is resolved through native geometry IDs, including table boxes, floor, robot/gripper and the other objects. At frame 10, ketchup, orange juice and milk have **no contacts with any recorded geometry**, not merely no table contacts, in every run. No direct or object-mediated support chain is present in those snapshots.
- At frame 0, ketchup/table contacts have separation as low as −0.02918 m; orange juice and milk/table contacts reach −0.02651 m. These are recorded collision penetrations, not inferred root-height gaps. Initial velocities are zero. The first settling endpoint has upward free-joint velocities about 3.154 m/s for ketchup and 2.816–2.822 m/s for orange juice/milk. The reference initial milk qacc is strongly upward despite downward gravity.
- At the pre-policy boundary those objects are descending. Reference root z values are approximately 1.05368 m (ketchup) and 0.89212 m (orange juice/milk), with vertical velocities −1.312 and −1.628 m/s respectively. Full collision bounds, rather than roots alone, lie well above table collision faces. The same finding persists in separately derived endpoint geometry at the recorded qpos time.
- The reference camera sheet shows the corresponding rise, loss from the camera field at height, descent and return. This corroborates, rather than substitutes for, geometry and dynamics evidence. The reference first later table contact is frame 14 (0.7 s) for orange juice/milk and frame 16 (0.8 s) for ketchup.

**Recommended technical interpretation:** these are actual transient free-flight configurations during incomplete settling, following penetration in the selected initial configuration. They are not floating objects held by another support, static constraints, a geometry-ID offset, or the millimetre-scale observation timing discrepancy. Contact-resolution launch is the best-supported dynamics explanation; no per-contact impulse history is stored to decompose its exact impulse, and no robot-intention or policy-contact cause is asserted. The policy has not issued its first action during settling.

The saved XML also puts named initialization-region sites at world z=0.41, while the upper faces of the relevant table collision boxes are at z=0.43676: a 0.02676 m difference. This is read from saved site declarations and collision transforms, never object roots. Together with the actual initial penetrations it identifies a setup/scene consistency problem worth retaining. The region plane is not itself a support surface, and the historical reason that the selected archive configuration penetrates this collision model cannot be proved from the recordings alone. It must not be attributed to a specific library revision without evidence.

Evidence: `settling_findings_by_run.json`, `targeted_measurements.json/.csv`, `constraints_and_identities.json`, `initial_sites_vs_collision_geometry.json`, `prepolicy_static_endpoint_geometry.json`, and `examples/episode_000_sheet.png`. The full per-frame contact owner, separation and location are retained; direct and indirect support are not conflated.

## 3. cream_cheese_1 in episode_002

At frame 0 cream cheese contacts table geom 10 with approximately −0.000696 m separation and also overlaps orange-juice geom 144 with approximately −0.003582 m separation. After the first settling command its vertical velocity is about +1.420 m/s and its position moves laterally. The camera sheet shows it lifting, moving toward/outside the table edge and disappearing below the table view.

By frame 7 (0.35 s), its collision shape is laterally beyond the upper table-face union; by frame 8 (0.4 s), the entire recorded shape is below the table top. At frame 10 (0.5 s), collision z bounds are approximately 0.15933–0.19454 m, with no table, object, gripper or floor contact. Floor contact first appears in frame 12 (0.6 s). Its free-body definitions contain no holding constraint. The separate endpoint-qpos reconstruction confirms that the object is already off the table at the clarified start.

**Recommended interpretation:** a real setup departure/descent, with initial object interpenetration as relevant evidence. A later floor contact during policy execution does not establish that departure occurred during policy execution. This is not a missed table contact caused by the lossy name list. Initial object interaction is supported by the record, but a detailed causal impulse allocation is unverified. Do not exclude this run or count it as a policy-phase requirement violation automatically. Preserve it as a setup-anomaly and interval-boundary example with pending human interpretation.

If initial support is interpreted strictly at the saved pre-policy state, the three elevated objects do not have the intended initial table-support configuration; they must not be silently treated as protected-and-passed. ## 5. Measurement timing: what is established and what remains

Producer-hash-verified sources establish this order: set the saved initial state and force observations; save frame 0; for each settling/policy action call env.step; obtain raw observations; after success checking, write the snapshot's raw observations, copy simulator state/derived arrays and contacts, then write the transition. No physics step occurs inside Recorder.snapshot. The recorder's raw-image writes do not create fresh camera measurements.

The recorded model uses Euler integration and h=0.002 s. Version-matched [MuJoCo 3.8.1 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/3.8.1/src/engine/engine_forward.c) computes kinematics/collision during forward dynamics before integration. This upstream reference supports the ordering explanation; it is not a hash attestation of the historical installed binary.

For all 7,965 protected object/frame cases examined here, free-joint translations satisfy `qpos_endpoint − recorded_body_position = h × qvel_endpoint` to at most 1.11e−16 m. Separately undoing that configuration increment with mj_integratePos(−h) and applying only mj_kinematics reproduces body positions to 1.39e−17 m and quaternions to 6.67e−16 component error. This supports the recorded body/geometry cache being the last pre-integration configuration, nominally t−h, rather than endpoint qpos at t. Static methods and arrays are saved separately in `static_reconstructions.jsonl`; they are not replacement trajectories, and no contact solving or dynamics advancement occurred.

Raw object positions fit a further-substep sampling hypothesis closely: `raw ≈ qpos_endpoint − 2h*qvel_endpoint + h²*qacc`. Across targeted cases the maximum residual is 2.50e−7 m; among cases with no snapshot contacts it is 2.50e−8 m. This explains the earlier up-to-6.50 mm representation difference without making that difference a tolerance. It is not an exact time stamp: damping/integration details and sensor caches remain relevant.

Current robosuite base.py, observables.py and robot_env.py show per-physics-step observable updates, cached values returned without force refresh, and camera rendering through sampled observables at the control sampling rate. Their historical file hashes are absent. Consequently the inferred raw-position phase (roughly t−2h in these records) and camera phase must not be presented as producer-verified capture timestamps. Raw positions and images are recorded in the same observation dict, but that alone does not prove identical capture instants for all observables.

Defensible bundles for the future checker:

| Bundle | Permitted use | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded body_xpos/body_xquat, geom_xpos/geom_xmat, contact IDs/distances/positions | Coherent geometric/contact snapshot at the stored last forward-pass configuration | Nominal phase t−h is inferred and supported by the targeted reconstruction; test identity/geometry consistency before using further frames. No extra substep coverage is created. |
| Recorded endpoint qpos and qvel | Endpoint configuration and velocity, with correct native joint layout | Do not combine endpoint qpos geometry with earlier contact records as if simultaneous. qacc is a dynamics-evaluation quantity, not automatically the endpoint velocity derivative under implicit damping. |
| Separate static geometry from unmodified endpoint qpos | Inspect initial/terminal endpoint geometry and bracket coverage | No contacts are recomputed; saved contact lists remain earlier evidence. Never overwrite stored poses or claim the static result is an observed extra frame. |
| Raw object positions/quaternions | Policy input/observation evidence with a qualified cache phase | Do not combine directly with cached simulator contact geometry as a simultaneous state. |
| Existing camera images | Human corroboration of configuration and motion sequence | Frame labels give host/simulator snapshot references, not independent exposure times. Occlusion and historical camera-cache identity limit exact cross-modal timing. |

The clarified interval still begins at saved frame 10 and ends at the terminal snapshot. A recorded pose cache nominally t−h does not cover the final 2 ms of the endpoint state geometrically; a separate static endpoint configuration may help bracket that boundary but cannot recover transient contacts. Continuous physics coverage is absent even though stored control endpoints are complete at 20 Hz.

## 6. Geometry-ID and rotation correction, integrated as a new downstream interface

This builds on the prior diagnosis; it does not redo the name-collapse investigation. `corrected_native_geometry_v2.json` lists all 161 native IDs, including each anonymous table geom. It binds the XML hash and all 50 run IDs; body/joint names are checked against each saved index. It covers table IDs 9–15 (visual mesh 9, collision boxes 10–15), floor ID 8, protected objects, target objects, basket, robot/gripper and other scene surfaces. The derived `corrected_entity_inventory_v2.json` contains corrected entity IDs without copying legacy numerical geom_ids.

`src/native_geometry_v2.py` is the versioned downstream entry point. It rejects a legacy name-list record, incomplete IDs, out-of-range contacts and a mismatching run/XML identity. Consumers use `by_id`/`contact_pair`, not list indexing through simulator_index.geom_names. The old collector/inventory is retained for provenance and must not be used for native geometry-ID lookup. The package's source and tests exercise the actual recorded failure: legacy index 10 says robot0_g0_vis, but native geom 10 is anonymous table collision geometry; robot0_g0_vis is native 16. Legacy index 149 says basket_1_g0, while native 149 is milk_1_g1. This correction is integrated into the versioned derived inventory/API for the next checker, without modifying a pilot input or the original inventory.

The rotation residual is explained quantitatively: for all 80 serialized box-geometry quaternions checked, the relative rotations recovered from recorded body/geom matrices round to the saved XML quaternion tokens at six significant digits. Recompiling those rounded tokens gives the earlier approximately 1.86e−6 rotation-component residual. The resulting conservative box-corner displacement bound is at most 7.28e−7 m over all boxes and 6.06e−8 m for the protected-object/table/floor subset; it is much smaller than setup penetrations but must still be represented honestly in precision claims.

The initial rounding diagnostic suppresses quaternion recovery noise below 1e−12 only when displaying algebraic zeros; it does not change any geometry/pose. A focused test repeats every comparison with the tighter 512 × epsilon allowance and obtains the same 80/80 matches. The rounding explanation therefore does not depend on that more conservative display cutoff.

The corrected map retains both XML and separately derived runtime local transforms. Composing the latter with recorded body poses across all targeted frames gives maximum errors 3.34e−16 m (positions) and 2.45e−15 (rotation components). The arithmetic consistency allowance is **512 × binary64 epsilon × coordinate scale**, using scale at least 1 for positions, and 512 × epsilon for rotation components. This is a conservative operation-budget allowance for small matrix/quaternion compositions, cross-checked by the residuals; it is not a scene clearance, contact, support, persistence or fall threshold. It must not absorb the 6.50 mm phase difference. Recorded per-frame geom poses remain the preferred geometry evidence; nothing is silently replaced.
